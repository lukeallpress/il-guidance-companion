# Illinois AI Guidance: Proposed Role-Based Reading Paths

**Source:** Illinois State Board of Education, *Artificial Intelligence Guidance*, June 2026  
**Official landing page:** https://www.isbe.net/AIGuidance  
**Official Quick Start FAQ:** https://www.isbe.net/Pages/AI-Guidance-FAQ.aspx  
**Source PDF:** `AIGuidance.pdf` (408 PDF pages)

## Purpose

This document proposes a navigational layer through the ISBE guidance. It does not create, revise, summarize, endorse, or supplement AI guidance.

The eventual site should:

- Preserve ISBE's section titles, question titles, answers, wording, and page references.
- Use ISBE's own Quick Start FAQ audience tags as the evidence base for role routing.
- Recommend an order in which a reader encounters the official material.
- Distinguish clearly between ISBE content and the site's editorial navigation.
- Link every card or route step back to an exact location in the official PDF.
- Avoid presenting a reading path as an additional policy recommendation.

The phrase **reading path** means only "an ordered set of official ISBE sections for a particular audience."

## What ISBE's Existing Site and FAQ Establish

ISBE already provides two important parts of the intended experience:

1. The landing page frames the guidance around four tenets and Illinois-specific, context-sensitive use.
2. The Quick Start FAQ lets readers filter questions by **tenet** and **role**, with each answer pointing to sections of the full guidance.

The proposed site should extend that structure rather than reproduce it unchanged. Its distinct value should be:

- A staged journey: **Start Here -> First Pass -> Deep Dive -> Reference Shelf**.
- More useful central-office lanes, especially technology/operations and teaching/learning.
- Grade-band routes for educators, students, and student-support staff.
- Stable "send this to..." handoff bundles for cross-functional work.
- Persistent anchors into the PDF and into the site's route pages.

ISBE also states in the PDF that different readers will want big-picture, legal, policy, or classroom entry points and recommends the summaries at the beginning of longer sections as a way into the document (pp. 25-26). The staged routes below operationalize that existing direction.

## Recommended Entry Model

### First question: What is your role?

Use eight top-level role cards.

| Site role | Includes | Basis in ISBE's FAQ audience tags |
|---|---|---|
| 1. District/System Executive | Superintendent, cabinet, senior system leadership | Superintendent; central office administration |
| 2. Technology, Data, and Operations | CTO/CIO, technology director, data/privacy/security, procurement, operational systems | Central office administration, narrowed through the concepts, application-review, governance, vendor, security, and privacy sections |
| 3. Curriculum, Instruction, and Assessment | Academic leadership, curriculum, assessment, instructional technology, professional learning | Central office administration, narrowed through teaching and learning, AI literacy, grade-band, and assessment sections |
| 4. School/Building Leader | Principal, assistant principal, dean, building leadership | Principal |
| 5. Teacher/Educator | Classroom teacher, instructional coach, librarian/media specialist | Teacher |
| 6. Student Support, Equity, and Accessibility | Special education, multilingual education, gifted education, counseling, MTSS, SEL, accessibility | Central office administration; teacher; principal; advocate/stakeholder group, narrowed through student services and special-population sections |
| 7. Student | Student readers and student advisory groups | Student |
| 8. Family, Board, and Community | Parent/caregiver, board member, advocate, stakeholder group, community member | Parent/caregiver; board of education; advocate/stakeholder group; community |

The eighth card should immediately branch into three subroutes:

- Family/caregiver
- Board/governance
- Advocate/community stakeholder

These readers share some orientation material but should not receive one undifferentiated path.

### Second question: What are you focused on?

Offer this as an optional filter after role selection:

- Shared understanding and readiness
- Teaching, learning, and assessment
- AI literacy and digital citizenship
- Policy, governance, and accountability
- Tool selection, procurement, privacy, and security
- Student well-being, accessibility, and special populations
- Stakeholder and family engagement
- Grade band: PreK-2, Grades 3-5, Grades 6-8, Grades 9-12

### Do not use these as first-level filters

- Rural, suburban, or urban
- Large, medium, or small district
- Public or private

The guidance acknowledges major variation in district configuration, capacity, resources, and location, but it does not provide separate bodies of content for those categories. Local context should shape how the guidance is used; it does not need to create separate reading routes.

For readers outside Illinois public school districts, the site can identify the source's stated Illinois district context without creating a separate "private school" interpretation.

## Stages Used in Every Reading Path

| Stage | Site behavior | Content rule |
|---|---|---|
| Start Here | Display the common orientation and the role's featured FAQ questions | Use official FAQ question titles and answers; do not paraphrase them into new guidance |
| First Pass | Show a short ordered sequence of section summaries and high-priority sections | Link to exact pages and retain official titles |
| Deep Dive | Expand to the complete role-relevant sections | Keep non-selected sections collapsed but available |
| Reference Shelf | Surface appendices, templates, matrices, cases, and resource tables | Label these as ISBE appendices/resources, not as site-created tools |
| Send Along | Create a stable cross-role bundle with a short neutral label | Link directly to the same official sections another role needs |

## Common Orientation for Every Role

Every route begins with:

1. **Quick Start FAQ introduction** - p. 4
2. **Welcome Letter** - pp. 25-26
3. **Start Here: How to Use This Guidance** - pp. 27-30

The role route then moves to the relevant section summaries rather than requiring a linear read of the entire document.

# Deep-Link and Anchor Model

## Site-owned canonical links

Use a stable site route as the shareable URL:

```text
/path/{role}?stage={stage}&focus={focus}&grade={grade}#{anchor-key}
```

Example:

```text
/path/technology-operations?stage=first-pass&focus=tool-review#application-review
```

The site can then open the official section in the hosted PDF:

```text
/AIGuidance.pdf#page=144
```

The `#page=` fragment works in many browser PDF viewers, but support varies on mobile devices and in-app browsers. The site-owned route should therefore remain the canonical share link, with the PDF page as the destination.

## Minimum route-step metadata

Each route step should eventually be represented with:

```json
{
  "role": "technology-operations",
  "stage": "first-pass",
  "sequence": 4,
  "anchor_key": "application-review",
  "source_title": "AI Application Review for Instruction and Operations and Goodness-of-Fit Example",
  "start_page": 144,
  "end_page": 158,
  "faq_questions": [28, 40, 43, 50],
  "send_to_roles": ["district-system-executive", "school-building-leader"],
  "source": "ISBE Artificial Intelligence Guidance, June 2026"
}
```

This metadata is navigational. It should not contain a generated interpretation of the guidance.

# Collapsed and Expanded Content Behavior

For a selected role:

- Expand the common orientation, the role's featured FAQ questions, and the current reading stage.
- Collapse other roles and later stages.
- Keep all official sections searchable and reachable.
- Show exact official section titles and page ranges before a reader opens a section.
- If the site displays a "quick hit," use the official FAQ question and answer or the official section-summary page. Do not generate a replacement summary.
- A reader should always be able to switch from a curated path to the full table of contents.
- A handoff link should open the recipient's route, not merely the sender's current page.

# Source-Fidelity Notes for Implementation

These details should be handled deliberately rather than silently corrected:

1. **The official web FAQ already filters by tenet and role.** The project should add staged paths and handoffs rather than merely clone that filter.
2. **Printed page numbers and PDF page numbers align.** The cover is PDF page 1; the page marked "2" is PDF page 2. A direct `#page=144` target reaches the page printed as 144.
3. **FAQ Q10 and Q7 have the same displayed question title but point to different material.** Preserve their question numbers and source mappings.
4. **FAQ Q23 and Q24 display the same question title and section mapping in the source.** Do not merge or delete one without explicitly treating it as source cleanup rather than navigation.
5. **The guidance uses both "Authorized Use Policy" and "Acceptable/Authorized Use Policy" language around AUP.** Preserve the exact title used at each source location.
6. **Grade-band labels vary slightly across the document** (`P-2`, `PK-2`, `PreK-Grade 2`, and hyphen/en-dash variations). A consistent UI filter is reasonable, but source titles should remain exact.
7. **Section summaries are the safest "quick hits."** They are already part of the official guidance and were explicitly designed as entry points.
8. **Resource listings are not endorsements.** Page 243 contains ISBE's own note that listed tools/resources are neither endorsed nor required. Keep that note visible whenever resource tables are surfaced.

# Recommended Initial Site Scope

The first useful release does not need every possible filter.

Build the content model around:

1. Eight role cards.
2. Four reading stages.
3. Grade-band selection where relevant.
4. The master source map and exact PDF page anchors.
5. The eight stable handoff bundles.
6. Official FAQ questions and answers as the quick-entry layer.

Add focus filters only after those routes are validated against the source and tested with representative readers.
